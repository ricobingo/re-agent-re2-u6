# Lesson Plan — Unit 6A, Lesson 1 (6A1)

**Course:** Reading Explorer 2 (3rd ed.) · **Unit 6:** Reef Encounters · **Reading A:** *Cities Beneath the Sea*
**Lesson type:** Vocabulary + Video (PPP) · **Focus:** 10 Reading A target words + video pre-training
**Mode:** Online, ClassIn · **Class:** 1 teacher : 4 students · **Time:** 60 minutes
**Companion:** student deck `6A1.html` (26 slides, project on screen) · teacher card `6A1-教师带课卡.html`

---

## 1. Aims

- **Main aim:** Students will be able to understand and use the ten Reading A target words (*brilliant, chemical, conservation, construct, negative, occupy, pollution, release, remarkable, shallow*) to talk about coral reefs and threats to them.
- **Subsidiary aims:** activate coral-reef background knowledge through the *Ocean Megabuilders* video; practise the collocations *a negative effect on* and *release … into*; predict the content of Reading A.
- **Personal aim:** keep the six spotlight words brisk (≤2 min each) so the production stage and the two before-you-read stages are not squeezed.

## 2. Assumptions

- Students have seen photos of coral reefs but do not know that coral is an animal, nor that reefs are built of limestone.
- Students watched the *Ocean Megabuilders* video before class (pre-training placement, Go35 rule). Some may not have.
- Students can type in the ClassIn chat and use the mouse tool on the shared browser page.

## 3. Anticipated problems & solutions

| Layer | Problem | Solution |
|---|---|---|
| Language | *brilliant* read as "clever" (the common dictionary sense students already know) | Present it only with the reef photo and the text sentence; CCQ "Bright or dull?"; do not translate as 聪明. |
| Language | *shallow* and *hollow* / *narrow* confused by sound | Board *deep ↔ shallow* as a pair; gesture; CCQ "Can you stand up in shallow water? (Yes)". |
| Language | *release* assumed to be positive ("release an animal") | Anchor it to the text use: fishermen *release* cyanide **into** the reef. Show both uses, mark the preposition. |
| Content | Students merge two numbers: reefs "50 million years" (Reading A caption) and the Great Barrier Reef "20,000 years" (video) | Teacher explicitly separates them at slide 7: one is about reefs in general, the other about one specific reef. |
| Task | One student answers, three hide | Every reveal is preceded by "everyone types first"; the retrieval games are click-per-student, rotate the mouse. |
| Online | Video did not get watched before class | If fewer than 3 of 4 watched, play 0:00–1:00 only (≤90 s) at slide 6, then continue; do not play the whole video in class. |

## 5. Materials

Student deck `6A1.html` (26 slides, opened in the ClassIn browser tool); *Ocean Megabuilders* video embedded on slide 6; ClassIn chat, mouse tool, timer.

## 6. Procedure (60 min)

| # | Stage (slide) | Min | Interaction | What happens | Answer key |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Lead-in: cover + unit journey (s1–s2) | 3 | T–Ss | Set the unit: reefs are cities built by tiny animals. Show the 4-lesson map. | — |
| 2 | Warm-up: what do you know? (s3) | 3 | Ind → T–Ss | Coursebook Warm Up questions; every student types one fact + one threat. | open |
| 3 | Warm-up game: guess the numbers (s4) | 4 | Ind → T–Ss | Students type a guess for each figure, then tap to reveal. | less than 1% · 25% · up to 99% |
| 4 | Video pre-teach (s5) | 3 | Ind → T–Ss | Match 4 video words to meanings. | habitat=b · colony=d · warn=a · stretch=c |
| 5 | Video revisit (s6) | 2 | T–Ss | Video was pre-watched; recall prompts only. Play ≤90 s if uptake was low. | — |
| 6 | Video comprehension (s7) | 3 | Ind → T–Ss | Three questions, everyone types, then reveal. | basketball · Great Barrier Reef ~20,000 yrs · ~500 million people |
| 7 | Video → reading bridge (s8) | 2 | T–Ss | Three facts recalled from memory, then reveal. | warm · polyps · limestone |
| 8 | Vocabulary overview (s9) | 1 | T–Ss | The ten words presented as a set: 6 to use, 4 to recognise. | — |
| 9 | Presentation: 6 spotlight words (s10–s15) | 12 | T–Ss | Photo first → students guess → reveal meaning → teacher asks the CCQ from §4 orally → read the text example aloud. | see §4 |
| 10 | Quick set: 4 recognition words (s16) | 3 | T–Ss | Fast presentation with the text example for each. | — |
| 11 | Practice: match all six (s17) | 4 | Ss (rotate mouse) | Word ↔ meaning for the six spotlight words. | in-deck |
| 12 | Practice: gap-fill (s18) | 4 | Ss | Four sentences rewritten from the text facts; tests the four recognition words. | occupy · pollution · release · conservation |
| 13 | Chunks aloud (s19) | 3 | T–Ss → Ind | Read the six chunks; each student puts one into their own sentence. | — |
| 14 | **Production** (s20) | 5 | Ind → T–Ss | ONE true sentence about their own world using TWO target words; teacher recasts, reads two aloud. | open (≥6 words, 2 target words) |
| 15 | Photo caption input (s21) | 1 | T–Ss | Read the coursebook p.84 caption together — it supplies the evidence for the next stage. | — |
| 16 | Before you read: true/false (s22) | 3 | Ind → T–Ss | Coursebook Before You Read A, answered from the caption. Wrong items corrected aloud. | T · F (animal) · F (larger) · T |
| 17 | Before you read: predict + vote (s23) | 2 | Ind | Vote on the biggest threat; each gives one reason. Teacher records the votes for 6A2. | open |
| 18 | **Final retrieval sprint** (s24) | 2 | Ss | All 10 words, timed — the lesson's only scored game, and its last retrieval. | in-deck |
| 19 | Bridge to 6A2 + exit (s25–s26) | 2 | T–Ss | Name the next skill (cause and effect); exit recap. | — |

**Total = 60 min** (rows sum to 60). Protect stages 9, 14, 16 and 18.

## 7. Differentiation (1:4)

- **Support:** for the gap-fill, give the first letter; for production, allow the model sentence pattern with one word changed.
- **Challenge:** in production, require two target words *plus* a because-clause; at s22, ask the student to say which paragraph of the coming reading will prove each statement.

## 8. Assessment evidence collected this lesson

- The CCQs in §4 are asked **orally by the teacher**; they are not printed on the student deck.
- Match, gap-fill and the final sprint → who still misses which word (feeds the "认得" column of the vocabulary ledger).
- Production sentences → first "会用" evidence for two words per student.
- The threat vote at s23 → reopened at the start of 6A2 as a prediction check.
