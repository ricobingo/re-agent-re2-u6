# Lesson Plan — Unit 6B, Lesson 2 (6B2)

**Course:** Reading Explorer 2 (3rd ed.) · **Unit 6:** Reef Encounters · **Reading B:** *The Truth About Great Whites*
**Lesson type:** Intensive reading (Receptive cycle) · **Focus:** contrastive relationships + fact vs speculation
**Mode:** Online, ClassIn · **Class:** 1 teacher : 4 students · **Time:** 60 minutes
**Companion:** student deck `6B2.html` (32 slides) · teacher card `6B2-教师带课卡.html` · **final lesson of Unit 6**

---

## 1. Aims

- **Main aim:** Students will be able to recognise contrastive relationships in a factual text through the signals *but, however, though/although/even though, despite/in spite of*, and to use one of them in their own written sentence.
- **Subsidiary aims:** distinguish stated fact from speculation using the text's own hedging phrases (*it is thought, they believe, according to this idea, perhaps*); consolidate the ten Reading B target words in context; identify which of two competing explanations the author favours and say how they know.
- **Personal aim:** leave a full four minutes for the writing stage — this is the unit's final written output and it must not be cut.

## 2. Assumptions

- Students met the ten target words in 6B1 and made a recorded prediction about how dangerous great whites are.
- Students can follow a narrative but tend to accept any sentence in a text as a stated fact.
- *But* and *however* are known; *despite* vs *even though* (noun vs clause) is not.

## 3. Anticipated problems & solutions

| Layer | Problem | Solution |
|---|---|---|
| Language | *despite* followed by a clause (*despite he was…*) | Board the contrast at the skill stage: *despite* + **noun**, *even though* + **clause**; slide 23 hints at each item's grammar. |
| Language | *though* at the end / middle of a sentence not recognised as a contrast signal | Slide 23 item 2 is exactly this position ("Surprisingly though,"); ask where the word sits. |
| Concept | Fact vs speculation judged by plausibility, not by the text's wording | Slide 24 sorts, then slide 25 names the giveaway phrases; require students to quote the phrase, not their opinion. |
| Concept | "The author prefers explanation 1 because it comes first" | Slide 29 addresses it head-on: the *However* that doubts explanation 1, and the fact that explanation 2 gets the last word. |
| Language | Pronoun *them* (slide 16) — the coursebook's own trap | Board the sentence and split it: *They* = sharks, *them* = people. |
| Task | The map question is unanswerable if the map is skipped | The map slide (s26) sits immediately before the comprehension slide; do not skip it. |

## 4. Target language

Recycled from 6B1 (all ten appear in the passage): *bite, confusion, flow, force, frightening, gather, horror, inaccurate, tempting, victim*.

New language taught this lesson:

| Item | Form | Note |
|---|---|---|
| but / yet | conjunction | joins two clauses |
| However, | sentence adverb | new sentence + comma |
| though / although / even though | subordinator | + a **whole clause** |
| despite / in spite of | preposition | + a **noun (phrase)** |
| it is thought / they believe / according to this idea / perhaps | hedges | signal speculation, not fact |

## 7. Differentiation (1:4)

- **Support:** at stage 10, tell students to look only at what follows the gap (noun or clause); at stage 17, allow the three starters verbatim.
- **Challenge:** at stage 11, ask for a rewrite that turns one speculation into a fact-style claim and say what evidence would be needed; at stage 17, require the contrast structure (*can … but rarely …*) inside the paragraph.

## 8. Assessment evidence collected this lesson

- Slide 23 accuracy → whether the noun/clause distinction has landed (re-tested in the homework and the U5+U6 quiz).
- Slide 24 sort → fact-vs-speculation as a scored strategy strand.
- Slide 30 paragraph → the unit's final written PRE output; feeds Part C of the homework and is the baseline for the quiz's unsupported writing task.
- The before/after vote (6B1 s3 → 6B2 s3 → s32) → visible evidence for parents that the lesson changed a belief, with the student's own reason attached.
