# Lesson Plan — Unit 6B, Lesson 1 (6B1)

**Course:** Reading Explorer 2 (3rd ed.) · **Unit 6:** Reef Encounters · **Reading B:** *The Truth About Great Whites*
**Lesson type:** Vocabulary (PPP) · **Focus:** 10 Reading B target words + the prefix *in-*
**Mode:** Online, ClassIn · **Class:** 1 teacher : 4 students · **Time:** 60 minutes
**Companion:** student deck `6B1.html` (24 slides) · teacher card `6B1-教师带课卡.html`

---

## 1. Aims

- **Main aim:** Students will be able to understand and use the ten Reading B target words (*bite, confusion, flow, force, frightening, gather, horror, inaccurate, tempting, victim*) to describe a frightening event and to talk about great white sharks.
- **Subsidiary aims:** use the prefix *in-* to form and understand *incomplete, incorrect, inexpensive*; distinguish the near-neighbours *frightening / tempting* and *flow / force*; predict the content of Reading B and record a class position to be tested next lesson.
- **Personal aim:** protect the production stage — do not let the memory game overrun.

## 2. Assumptions

- Students believe the popular image of the great white as a man-eater. The lesson deliberately leaves that belief standing; Reading B overturns it next lesson.
- Students know *bite* and *flow* passively but not their collocations (*bite with great force*, *blood flowing from*).
- *Victim* and *horror* may be known from films rather than from reading.

## 3. Anticipated problems & solutions

| Layer | Problem | Solution |
|---|---|---|
| Language | *frightening* vs *frightened* confused | Board the pair: the shark is frighten**ing**, the person is frighten**ed**; CCQ "Which one is the shark?" |
| Language | *tempting* taken as purely negative ("temptation = bad") | Present with the seal photo; CCQ "Do you WANT it? (Yes)"; elicit a tempting food from each student. |
| Language | *force* and *strength* used interchangeably | Anchor to the text collocation *bite with great force* and *the force of the wind* — force is power **applied**. |
| Language | Over-generalising *in-* to every adjective | State the limit explicitly: *in-* works on these three; do not invent *inbig, inhappy*. |
| Content | Students want the answers to the pre-reading true/false quiz now | Frame it as a bet: "Write your guess. Next lesson you check it yourself." Do not confirm. |
| Online | The memory game absorbs too much time | Cap it at 5 minutes on the timer; if unfinished, reveal remaining pairs and move on. |

## 7. Differentiation (1:4)

- **Support:** in the gap-fill, give the first letter; in production, allow the model sentence with one element changed.
- **Challenge:** in production, require a contrast (*It was frightening, but…*) — this pre-loads next lesson's skill; at stage 15, ask which continent has the largest protected area.

## 8. Assessment evidence collected this lesson

- Match / memory / sprint accuracy → "认得" evidence per word per student.
- Production sentences → "会用" evidence for two words per student.
- The recorded vote (s3) and the recorded true/false guesses (s22) are both reopened at the start of 6B2 — the lesson's own before/after measure.
