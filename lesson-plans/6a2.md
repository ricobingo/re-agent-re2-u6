# Lesson Plan — Unit 6A, Lesson 2 (6A2)

**Course:** Reading Explorer 2 (3rd ed.) · **Unit 6:** Reef Encounters · **Reading A:** *Cities Beneath the Sea*
**Lesson type:** Intensive reading (Receptive cycle: gist → detail → skill → response) · **Focus:** cause-and-effect relationships
**Mode:** Online, ClassIn · **Class:** 1 teacher : 4 students · **Time:** 60 minutes
**Companion:** student deck `6A2.html` (37 slides) · teacher card `6A2-教师带课卡.html`

---

## 1. Aims

- **Main aim:** Students will be able to identify cause-and-effect relationships in a factual text about coral reefs, using the signal words *as a result, consequently, because, so*, and to state one cause with its effect(s) in their own words.
- **Subsidiary aims:** consolidate the ten Reading A target words in context; practise pronoun reference and precise reading of hedged claims (*may, if*); produce a three-line PRE paragraph on which reef threat to fix first.
- **Personal aim:** hold each post-paragraph question to 90 seconds so that all nine paragraphs are actually read within the hour.

## 2. Assumptions

- Students met the ten target words in 6A1 and can recognise them; they have not yet used them for reading.
- Students can find explicit information in a paragraph but tend to treat any prominent sentence as the main idea.
- Cause-and-effect as a *reading* concept is new; students know the ideas in L1 but not the English signal words.

## 3. Anticipated problems & solutions

| Layer | Problem | Solution |
|---|---|---|
| Concept | Students assume the cause is always the first clause in the sentence | At the skill stage, board the two orders: *"Because of X, Y"* and *"Y, because of X"*; state the rule as **time**, not word order. |
| Concept | Students merge "threat" with "cause of bleaching" and pick any threat | Slide 24 makes all three options genuine threats from the text; the follow-up question forces them to justify from Paragraph H only. |
| Language | *release* mapped only to its positive sense ("release an animal") | Slide 19 contrasts the two uses; the collocation *release … into* is boarded. |
| Language | Pronoun *them* (slide 21) read as the nearest noun | Ask the actor/receiver question: "Who collects? Who gets collected?" |
| Skill | Students read *may* and *if* in the UN report as a statement of fact | Slide 25 is built on exactly this; circle the two words on screen and ask "Has it happened yet?" |
| Task | The 90-second limit slips and paragraphs G–I get cut | Paragraphs D–E and G–H are already paired on single slides to buy time; if behind at slide 22, drop slide 33 (sort), not the reading. |

## 4. Target language

Recycled from 6A1 (all ten appear in the passage): *brilliant, chemical, conservation, construct, negative, occupy, pollution, release, remarkable, shallow*.

New language taught this lesson:

| Item | Type | Note |
|---|---|---|
| as a result / consequently / therefore | discourse markers | effect follows |
| because (of) / as a result of | discourse markers | cause follows |
| so | conjunction | effect follows |
| caused / resulted in / was the reason for | verbs | cause is the subject |
| un- / -ful / re- | affixes | *uncounted, colorful, rebuild* — from the coursebook's own affix task |

## 7. Differentiation (1:4)

- **Support:** for the causal chain, give the first card; for the writing frame, allow the sentence starters verbatim.
- **Challenge:** at stage 14, ask for one cause–effect pair expressed with *consequently*; at stage 16, require a drawback that names who loses (locals / tourists / conservationists).

## 8. Assessment evidence collected this lesson

- Post-paragraph question accuracy per student → which reading action is weak (main idea vs reference vs precise reading).
- The two spoken productions (s20, s29) → "会用" evidence for *release* and one recycled word.
- The s36 paragraph → first written PRE evidence for the unit; feeds Part C of the homework.
